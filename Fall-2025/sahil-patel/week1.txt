-- CH7: Latency & Throughput --

Key ideas
- Latency: time from input arrival to its corresponding output being valid.
- Throughput: results per unit time (how fast outputs come out steady-state).
- Pipelines overlap work; the slowest stage caps the rate.

Core formulas
- Unpipelined comb:
  latency = t_PD
  throughput = 1 / t_PD

Definitions
- K-stage pipeline: every input→output path crosses exactly K registers.
- Convention: put a register at each stage output.

Basic method to pipeline
1) Draw a cut line at each stage boundary (every output must cross a line).
2) Ensure signals cross lines in the same direction.
3) Insert registers on stage outputs to enforce timing.

Interleaving (circuit-level “multi-copy”)
- Duplicate slow unit(s) and alternate inputs across copies to boost rate.
- Can combine with pipelining; bottleneck may shift elsewhere.

-- CH14: Memory Basics --

SRAM
- Cell: 2 cross-coupled inverters + 2 access transistors (bistable).
- Read: precharge bitlines to VDD; enable wordline; one bitline discharges slightly; sense amp detects.
- Write: drive bitlines, assert wordline, overpower cell to new state.
- Fast, bigger area per bit.

DRAM
- Cell: 1 transistor + 1 capacitor (very dense).
- Capacitor leaks > requires refresh.
- Slower than SRAM but far smaller area/bit.

Flash
- Floating-gate transistor holds charge; very dense; slower writes/erases.

-- CH15: 

5-stage datapath
- IF (fetch), RF (decode/read), ALU (execute), MEM (access), WB (write back)

Hazard handling
- Stall: simple, increases CPI.
- Bypass/forward: more hardware, reduces stalls/CPI.
- Speculation: predict to keep pipeline busy; recover on mispredict.

Notes
- Deeper pipelines encounter hazards more frequently.
- Compilers can reorder to separate dependent ops and reduce stalls.
