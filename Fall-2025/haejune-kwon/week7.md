## Benes network
- The common understanding of output-input connection of Benes network is as shown in "Benes_N32.png". This is how the stages are commonly thought to be connected like. Therefore, I used the same logic to connect the inputs and outputs for my Benes network. However, I did not get the desired result. So, I checked if the permutation is actually done in the same way for the "controlbits.pdf". I did some debugging to find out that the logic was actually different.
- The logic used in the pdf was:
stage 1: [i] & [i+1]
stage 2: [i] & [i+2]
stage 3: [i] & [i+4]
stage 4: [i] & [i+8]
stage 5: [i] & [i+16]
stage 6: [i] & [i+8]
stage 7: [i] & [i+4]
stage 8: [i] & [i+2]
stage 9: [i] & [i+1]
where two terms connected with with & are the terms put into the 2x2 crossbar. If the index was used already, it's not considered as the [i]th term. For example, if we used the terms [0] and [5] for stage 3, [5] was used, so it will skip [5] as we increment [i]. Therefore, using stage 3, [i] will increment like 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 11, and so on. This increment goes on until the second term reaches [31] (the last term for N=32).
- The control bits generated by the Python code from "conrtolbits.pdf" is given as 0th index on the left-most, which is the opposite of how systemverilog operates. Therefore, the bits need to be reversed when putting in the Benes network module.
